For your Airbnb price prediction, these are the strongest examples.

1. Clear nonlinear relationships → prefer nonlinear models

Suppose your EDA shows:

bedrooms vs price curves upward
bathrooms vs price curves upward nonlinearly
accommodates vs price has a nonlinear trend

That is evidence that a simple linear model may be restrictive.

Then your reasoning can be:

EDA
↓
Nonlinear relationships observed
↓
OLS linearity assumption may be too restrictive
↓
Evaluate tree-based / nonlinear models
↓
Random Forest / Gradient Boosting / XGBoost

This is one of your strongest arguments.

But say "suggests", not "proves."

2. Strong interactions / different effects across groups → tree models become attractive

Suppose price behaves differently depending on:

accommodates × room_type
bedrooms × neighbourhood
bathrooms × property_type

For example, 4 bedrooms in an entire home may have a very different pricing pattern from 4 bedrooms in a private room.

That suggests interactions.

A basic linear model without interaction terms won't automatically capture:

bedrooms×room_type

XGBoost and other tree-based methods can capture many such interactions automatically.

So your reasoning could be:

"Price appeared to depend on combinations of property characteristics rather than individual features alone, motivating nonlinear ensemble models capable of modelling feature interactions."

That's much stronger than saying "XGBoost is popular."

3. Large target skew / extreme prices → model and metric choice matter

If your price distribution looks like:

lots of low/moderate prices
↓
small number of extremely expensive listings

then you should expect:

RMSE to be strongly affected by extreme errors
linear regression coefficients to be sensitive to extreme observations
residuals to potentially be highly non-normal
prediction errors to vary with price level

This can motivate investigating:

log transformation of price
robust approaches
tree-based models
MAE alongside RMSE

It doesn't automatically mean XGBoost is best.

4. Outliers and influential observations → model robustness becomes a consideration

If your OLS diagnostics show substantial influential observations:

Cook's Distance
+
large residuals
+
high-leverage observations

then that is evidence that OLS may be sensitive to a small number of listings.

That can motivate comparing OLS against models such as:

Random Forest
Gradient Boosting
XGBoost

But again, Cook's Distance doesn't prove XGBoost is better. You need the model comparison to establish that.

5. Weak individual relationships → multivariate models become more important

Your accommodates example is actually useful here.

The scatter shows:

huge price variation at the same accommodation level.

That tells you accommodates alone is insufficient.

That does not mean "use XGBoost."

It means:

Price appears to depend on multiple variables simultaneously.

Then you can motivate a multivariate model, and EDA can further show whether the relationships are linear or nonlinear.

What would actually change your model choice?

I'd use this logic:

EDA finding	Modeling implication
Mostly linear relationships	Linear regression is reasonable baseline
Strong nonlinear relationships	Add nonlinear models
Important feature interactions	Tree-based/ensemble models become attractive
Strong multicollinearity	Linear model needs attention; regularization may help
Heavy target skew	Consider log-transform/robust methods
Many influential observations	Compare OLS with less sensitive alternatives
High-dimensional sparse categorical data	Consider regularized/boosting approaches
Weak univariate relationships but strong multivariate structure	Use multivariate models; don't discard features based only on correlation
